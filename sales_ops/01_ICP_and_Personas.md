# ICP & Buyer Personas

Grounded in Buyer Playbook v3 §2 ("Who Actually Buys"). This doc adds the disqualification criteria and the two decision-maker profiles you'll actually be selling to.

## Ideal Customer Profile (ranked)

### #1 — Independent Western lab, 5–30 employees, family-owned, no portal
- **Signal:** static PDF Rx forms on site, WhatsApp/email intake, "about us" mentions a founder by name, no Dandy/LabStar/Magic Touch branding.
- **Willingness:** $10–15k one-time (Standard tier).
- **Why:** feeling Dandy pressure but can't stomach a $2k/mo subscription; wants ownership + brand.
- **Decision speed:** 4–8 weeks from first call.

### #2 — UAE / GCC lab, modern brand, form-based intake
- **Signal:** Next.js/modern stack, marketing-forward site, "200+ partner clinics" hero copy, no client portal login.
- **Willingness:** $15–25k (Standard or Enterprise).
- **Why:** highest per-lab spend globally, brand-conscious, English-speaking.
- **Decision speed:** 3–6 weeks.

### #3 — India mid-market lab, 500+ partner dentists, DIY portal attempt
- **Signal:** site mentions "online dashboard" that doesn't work, dated ASP.NET portal, multi-channel intake (STL page + WhatsApp + Gmail) that doesn't unify.
- **Willingness:** ₹4–8L ($5–10k, Starter).
- **Why:** already believes in portals; easier yes than a first-time buyer.
- **Decision speed:** 2–4 weeks (fastest).

### #4 — PE-consolidator ops/integration lead
- **Signal:** LinkedIn title contains "Integration", "Operations", "IT" at a rollup (Catalis, Denbright, Standard Dental). Company acquired ≥2 labs in last 18 months.
- **Willingness:** $20–25k (Enterprise) — trivial next to their acquisition budget.
- **Why:** needs a common tech stack across newly-acquired labs, no time to build.
- **Decision speed:** 8–14 weeks (procurement cycle, but check size is high).

### #5 — Dental-tech startup buying a feature
- **Signal:** small team building an aligner/scan platform, 3D viewer or annotation is on their roadmap.
- **Willingness:** $15–25k (feature acquisition).
- **Why:** buy vs build math. 6 months of dev = $75k+.
- **Decision speed:** 4–8 weeks.

## Disqualify immediately (from Playbook §3 anti-prospects, generalized)
- Already runs a portal (`portal.*`, `login.*`, `.labstar.com`, "24/7 client portal" in marketing copy).
- Part of a lab group (ALS Dental, Modern Dental, National Dentex) — decisions happen at group HQ, not the lab.
- Site mentions DAMAS + MHRA compliance and "custom in-house app" — they've invested; they'll defend.
- Solo technician, no dentists on partner list.
- Contact form goes to a `gmail.com` / `yahoo.com` address AND site was last updated 5+ years ago — dormant.

## Two decision-maker personas inside every deal

### Persona A: "The Owner-Operator" (India + independent West)
- 45–65 years old, family owns the lab, kids may or may not want to take over.
- Cares about: brand, legacy, "not looking small next to Dandy".
- Doesn't care about: recurring subscription mechanics, SaaS terminology.
- **Buys because of:** perceived permanence (ownership > rental), a working demo they can touch, a peer they trust (reference).
- **Doesn't buy when:** you talk tech stack, use acronyms (SaaS, RLS, MRR), or price by seat.

### Persona B: "The Integration Lead" (PE consolidator, dental-tech co)
- 30–45 years old, ops/product background, MBA or engineering.
- Cares about: deployment risk, source access, SLA, roadmap alignment, exit clause.
- **Buys because of:** clean architecture, source code, being able to fire you and keep running.
- **Doesn't buy when:** you can't answer security questions cleanly, your license is exclusive-only, you insist on hosting.

## Qualification framework: BANT-lite for $10–15k

| Check | Green | Yellow | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Budget** | Named a number ≥ $8k in first call | "We'd have to see" | "How much is it?" as opener |
| **Authority** | Owner/CEO on call OR ops lead with sign-off named | "I'll check with my brother/partner" | "I'm just gathering info for my boss" |
| **Need** | Named a specific broken workflow (Rx PDFs, WhatsApp) | Nodded when you described one | Said "we're doing fine, just curious" |
| **Timeline** | Named a driver (Dandy renewal, new hire, new location) | Vague "next quarter" | "Someday" |

Three greens → send proposal. Two greens + specific need → schedule demo. One green → nurture. Zero → drop.

## First-call red flags (walk-away signals)
- Asks for exclusive license before agreeing to any price.
- Wants to pay in installments over 12 months on a $10k deal — cash flow problem, not a real buyer.
- "Can we do equity instead of cash?" — no. You already have equity from the anchor JV.
- Says "we can build this ourselves in a month" — they can't, but the conversation is over.
- Refuses to name their current intake workflow — hiding either that they already have a solution or that they're not the buyer.

## What "warm" actually means at this stage
- India: an intro from your anchor clinical partner, or a peer-of-a-peer inside the digital dentistry circle.
- West: a shared connection on LinkedIn (2nd degree) at a peer lab. Not a cold email.
- Best warmth: someone who's seen the Loom demo and asked to see it live.
