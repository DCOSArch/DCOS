## 2. Indian Statutory Patentability Compliance (Section 3(k) Analysis)

Under Section 3(k) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970, *“a mathematical or business method or a computer programme per se or algorithms”* are non-patentable. However, as clarified by the Delhi High Court (*Ferid Allani v. Union of India*, *Telefonaktiebolaget LM Ericsson v. Intex*) and the CRI Guidelines (2017):

1. **Technical Problem Solved:** The inventions solve specific physical operatory bottlenecks (scanner format fragmentation, clinical data tampering, CAD manufacturing misfits).
2. **Technical Effect & Tangible Transformation:** Inventions produce concrete physical effects (e.g., 3D mesh vertex reduction for sub-80ms WebGL rendering, automated milling soft-copy warranty binding, ECDH Curve25519 hardware key encapsulation).
3. **Hardware Interaction:** Interfacing with intraoral scanner hardware directories and physical CAD/CAM milling pipelines beyond generic computing.
